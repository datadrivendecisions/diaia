AI for SMEs

One team, two modules, one handbook. In AIBS the team researches how regional manufacturing SMEs can put AI to work; in AEL the same team builds the platform it does that research with.

This is the course site for the two modules. It holds the handbook the cohort writes, and — as the course takes shape — the weekly programme and the shared quality bar every team is measured against.

Two modules, one team

AIBS — AI in Business & SocietyAEL — Agent Engineering Lab
The trackThe research track: applied research on the business, economic and societal side of AI for SMEsThe technical track: building the research platform the team researches and writes with
Your roleIndependent researcherOne of four build roles — product manager, developer, tester, deployer
What you produceThe team's handbook contribution: the problem analysis, the sources and field data, the adviceThe team's own research platform, judged on whether it works — the handbook never depends on it
How it is assessedOne joint criterion-based interview, with a separate mark per module

The handbook

AI for SMEs — Handbook — a living handbook for the region's manufacturing SMEs, written by the cohort and compounding from year to year. What is there now is the template: the shell every team fills, with the chapter anatomy and the quality bar every chapter is measured against.

The programme

The weekly programme for both tracks, side by side: the theme of the week, the research assignment, the platform element that week adds, and the gate at the end of it.

The shared quality and design bar — the criteria every team's contribution and every team's platform are measured against, published before week 1 and unchanged during the cohort.

For the module owners

The integrated learning-requirements document specifies both modules — outcomes, calendar, the gated learning path and the assessment. It is written for the course team, not for students.
